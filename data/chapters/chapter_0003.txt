      Jamie strolled along to the back of the house where the break in had happened. The private detective whoever he was, hadn’t arrived yet and he wanted to have a good look in the crime scene. The forensics team were still working inside Whitmore’s room where the window had been broken.
      This was what he had imagined detective work would be like—picking apart crime scenes, piecing together scattered clues, and, at the perfect moment, swooping in with a brilliant deduction to unmask the culprit. It was the kind of thing he’d read about as a kid, the stuff of sharp-eyed detectives and thrilling mysteries.
      Then he’d actually joined the force.
      Turns out, most of the job wasn’t about unraveling fiendish plots—it was about paperwork, chasing down petty thieves who couldn’t outrun their own bad decisions, and, on one memorable occasion, recovering a missing parrot that had sworn at its owner before making a break for it. Hardly the stuff of legends.
      But this case… this was different. It had all the hallmarks of something big. And for once, Jamie wasn’t going to be the guy standing in the background nodding while someone else did the solving.
      A shattered window. A man dead in his bead.
      On paper, it seemed like a break in gone wrong. Some thief observed the family going out together and decided to try his luck. Daring, probably drunk he ventures in and finds a person actually present inside it. Panicking he kills him when he sees him. 
      Except thieves don’t leave empty-handed.
      And a murder at the heat of the moment was far from the actual death.
      A pill overdose.
      Or maybe the death and break-in were two different things totally Jamie thought. He shook his head. Nothing made sense.
      The window was impossible to access from the outside, unless the killer was twelve feet tall, which didn’t seem too probable or used a ladder. A ladder, which seemed conveniently placed in the toolshed nearby, had been thoroughly examined. It was spotless without a single mark or smudge, or anything that might indicate it had been used recently. Almost too clean Jamie thought as if someone had deliberately cleaned it after it’s use. And the toolshed again had been left unlocked as the gardener had lost its key just two days back. Again, almost too convenient. It was almost as if someone wanted to think it was a break-in gone wrong. Or had inside help.
      He walked nearby to look at the ground under the window, looking for some marks or anything. The ground stared back stolidly at him. It was hard and unyielding, and there was no hope of finding any mark on it. He bent to one knee and tried to observe everything on the ground as he had seen detectives do in movies. Looking for where a blade of grass was turned in a way it was not supposed to, and whatnot. Except there was no blade of grass down there to look at. After staring at the ground for a few minutes and feeling remarkably stupid, he got back up. Something brushed against his back. 
      Jamie let out an undignified yelp, jerking forward instinctively. He barely caught himself from toppling over, regaining his balance just in time to glare at the intruder. The intruder turned out to be a young man, more like a boy, who stood behind him. He was slight, thin with a sharp face, and in a black coat that seemed a few sizes too big. His face was pale, though his eyes were unerringly sharp and his hair a mop of unruly black hair that struck out in all sides.
      ‘Sorry,’ he said, stepping back apologetically ‘I didn’t mean to scare you.’ He had thought the area had been barred from the outside. Apparently, it was not.
      ‘You nearly gave me a heart attack kid,’  Jamie said, eyeing him up. ‘ Who are you anyway?’.
      ‘Sirius Wolfe.’
      ‘Oh, that explains everything,’ Jamie said in his driest tone.
      ‘Okay,’ he said before turning and observing the ground just like Jamie had a moment before.
      ‘That was sarcasm right there, kid.’ Jamie said laying a firm hand on his shoulder ‘And you better explain what you are doing in a crime scene before I throw you out.’ The young man, Sirius, didn’t reply, his eyes already far away as if Jamie was not there. Almost absent-mindedly he  muttered something under his breath,
      ‘What?’ Jamie snapped, getting annoyed.
      ‘The killer could not have gone up without a ladder’. he said, pointing at thewindow. Great Sherlock. You have figured that he is not twelve feet tall. ‘He couldn’t have brought a ladder with him all along the street without anyone noticing and gone back with it. The ladder in the shed was checked and came out clean. Also there is no way that an outside thief would know that the toolshed was unlocked if he was a member of the house or close to one of them anyway. And also, however meticulous a thief is a ladder placed against a white wall like this is bound to leave some marks on it. A smudge, a patch of dirt, or color something while leaning on it. The wall is clean as well and so is the ground. So it was not a random break-in that resulted in murder.’
      Jamie’s stomach did a small backflip. That had been exactly what he had been thinking. Though it had not been quite this intense. Sirius turned towards him.
      ‘Just who the hell are you’ he asked him in a hoarse voice.
      ‘You don’t know me,’ he frowned as if annoyed, ‘You were expecting some assistance in the case, weren’t you?’
      Jamie’s breath caught.
      No. No way.
      The young man extended his hand. ‘Nice to meet you, Jamie’ he said with a glance at his badge. ‘I’m Sirius Wolfe, the private detective.’
      Brilliant. Just brilliant.
