In the prologue of the chapter, we are introduced to Giselle, an artist who expresses herself through color, as she cannot hear the sounds of her environment. She paints a scene from her room, depicting the joyful movements of children, her brother Laurie, and others, each represented by specific colors that embody their essence. As she paints, she reflects on the emotions and personalities associated with each individual, noting their complexities. However, her creative process is disrupted when she inadvertently adds a striking red streak to her canvas, symbolizing distress or conflict, possibly evoked by the angry silhouette of Mr. Whitmore visible in the study window. Giselle calms herself with the thought that she can cover up any mistakes, illustrating a theme of concealment—suggesting that within her home, emotions and truths are often hidden beneath layers of paint and pretense.
