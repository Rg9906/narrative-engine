In the prologue of the story, we are introduced to Giselle, an artist who perceives the world through color rather than sound due to her profound deafness. As she prepares for a feast, she paints the people in her life on canvas, using colors that symbolize their essences and emotions: her brother Laurie radiates warmth, while Sebastian embodies quiet resignation, and Lucius is marked by grief. Talia pulses with vibrant energy, and Marlene's calm presence brings a comforting blue. A sudden interruption occurs when Mr. Whitmore, likely filled with anger, is seen shouting in silhouette, prompting an unintended streak of red on her canvas, which disturbs Giselle. Recognizing the mistake, she calms herself, deciding to paint over it in the morning, emphasizing her belief that nothing in her life cannot be concealed or managed with the right choice of color. The chapter sets the groundwork for themes of perception, emotion, and the layers people hide beneath their surfaces.
